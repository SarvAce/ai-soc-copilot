# Deviations and clarifications

Where `docs/POC_SPEC.md` (proposal v1.0.2) is silent or ambiguous, this file records
what the implementation does, why, and which section it concerns. It feeds proposal v1.1.

No ID, threshold, window, rule row, check or expected outcome has been changed.

| # | Spec section | What the spec says | What the build does | Why |

| D1 | B.2 (`Fact.fact_id`) | "stable hash of kind + params, shown as F07", but Figure 5 cites `F01`, `F02`, `F05`, `F09` | `fact_id` stays the stable hash and is both the merge key and what claims cite. Each `Fact` also carries a `label` (`F01`, `F02`, …) assigned per case in order of first appearance; only labels are shown in the UI and the narrative. | A hash cannot also be a two-digit ordinal. Keeping the hash as the merge key is what makes a second pass merge facts instead of duplicating them (B.1). | D17 | Table 19 (playbooks); Table 31 check R8; Figure 5 | The spec says a recommendation must cite "a playbook step valid for an active hypothesis", but does not say which of a hypothesis's steps a case offers | **New design — the playbook selection rule.** Candidates are the steps of the **highest active hypothesis** on the ladder GUESS < SPRAY < ATO, plus the one step flagged `source_containment` when a spray source is in scope (the source is the same whether the case stopped at spraying or became a takeover). Then: a step whose required fact is missing is withheld (`PB-ATO-04` "Review the MFA methods" is not offered when no MFA change was found); and **no `contain` step is offered on a LIKELY_BENIGN case**. Implemented in `core/playbook.py`. | Figure 5 shows the S2 case file recommending exactly four steps, which is fewer than the union of its two active hypotheses, so a rule is needed and the spec does not give one. These three clauses reproduce Figure 5 exactly and follow the domain: do not offer to revoke sessions on a case with no success, and do not offer containment on a case the rules call benign (domain rule 1, "a suspicious pattern is not proof"). Check R8 validates against this rule, so it is part of the gate, not cosmetic. Tested in `tests/test_playbook.py`. Approved 20 Oct 2026. |
| D18 | §15.1, Table 27 (R-SPRAY) | "One IP fails against at least 6 accounts within 30 minutes, with a median of at most 3 attempts per account"; §15.1 adds that "an alert covers the merged span of all windows that fire" | A sliding window qualifies on the **account count** alone. The **median-attempts condition is then judged over the merged span** of all qualifying windows, and the alert is dropped if the median exceeds the threshold. | Judging the median per window let a loud multi-account burst through: six accounts at four attempts each over twenty minutes always contains a narrower sub-window holding only three attempts each, which passes a median of 3 and fires R-SPRAY. Since §15.1 defines the alert as the merged span, the merged span is what has to look like a spray. This keeps R-SPRAY separate from R-GUESS, which is the reason the median rule exists. Regression test: `tests/test_detector.py::test_r_spray_median_rule_rejects_a_loud_burst`, with `test_r_spray_accepts_a_median_of_exactly_three` pinning the "at most" boundary. |
